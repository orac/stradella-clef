I'm Dan Hulme, a computer scientist and musician living in Cambridge, which has a lively accordion scene. I've played many instruments at different times in my life, but for the last ten years I've been learning to play accordion and writing accordion music. I gig a few times a year under the name Tufty Indigo.

When I started playing accordion, I already knew piano, so I didn't have to *learn bass clef* like people whose first instrument is accordion. Even so, I do a lot of sheet music, and I started to resent how much mental effort it takes 
 I've developed the Stradella clef notation over a period of a few years, and I've been writing and playing parts in its current form since 2024. In a way, I've been trying to solve two problems:&mdash;

1. Existing notation lets down players while we're playing, by making us work harder.
2. No music engraving systems *understand* the conventions of accordion sheets. LilyPond in particular prides itself on separating presentation (how to show the music) from meaning (what notes to play), but to engrave accordion parts you need to abuse octave marks and fingerings to show the chords, tying the music you input to one particular presentation.

Initially, I started using colour to augment the traditional "M", "m", "7" markings above the staff, but after playing from sheets with that format, I realised the letters had become unnecessary. And then when I observed that the top, middle, and bottom lines of a five-line staff had the same notes between them as the marked C, E, and A♭ buttons, the idea to write a tablature-like format came together.
